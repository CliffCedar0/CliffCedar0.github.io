Abstract

Retired electric-vehicle batteries may retain useful capacity, but decisions about repair, further testing, reuse, and recycling require an assessment that connects battery condition with supporting evidence. A state-of-health (SOH) estimate describes capacity retention, while provenance, uncertainty, missing information, and applicability determine how that estimate can inform a subsequent action. We present EVA-Batt, an information system for evidence-grounded explainability that implements this relation through a claim-level evidence contract. Three agents standardize battery management system (BMS) data, organize SOH evidence, and audit report claims. Typed records associate each claim with its source, measurement status, uncertainty, missing information, and decision boundary. A lightweight adapter trained on clean, counterfactual, and mutated cases converts these records into structured audit results for deterministic report rendering. We evaluate the complete workflow through a three-arm comparison involving 1,000 aligned records and 3,000 generated reports. The constrained arm achieved traceability and numeric fidelity scores of 1.0000, together with an Audit Credibility Score (ACS) of 0.5847. The two free-form arms achieved ACS values of 0.0198 and 0.0135, respectively. Event-level and perturbation tests further showed that the constrained workflow preserved source rows, uncertainty, and missing inputs when evidence was degraded. EVA-Batt therefore connects battery-health estimates with the evidence required to interpret and use them. ACS represents report inspectability under the EVA-Batt contract, while safety assessment and human decision quality remain separate evaluation targets.
