From a Second-Tier University to an MPhil at UNSW (Application Memoir: From a Chance Start to a Dream Realised)

Autobiography · 2026-10-23 · Junjie Chen

When I first planned my university life, studying abroad never really crossed my mind. My plan was straightforward: enter competitions in my first two years, then prepare for China’s postgraduate entrance exams in my third and fourth years. I had no grand ambitions. I just hoped I could get into “福star”.

Things changed in the first semester of my third year. Our AI lecturer, C, had returned to China after completing a PhD at UNSW and teaching at UTS. He supervised master’s students at our university. I still remember what he said after our very first class: “If any of you are interested in research, come and talk to me. I have a project in collaboration with an overseas university, UNSW. It could also help with your postgraduate admissions interviews.”

I joined C’s research group thinking I would give it a try. I never expected that we would produce some results, or that I would publish my first academic paper as first author. It was only an EI-indexed conference paper, so go easy on me! But the feeling that my work had led to something concrete was enormously encouraging.

Later, C brought me into a meeting with a professor from UNSW. The whole conversation was in English. I could just about follow it, but getting my own thoughts out was a struggle. Luckily, I had a project to show that I had built and integrated into a working application myself. The UNSW professor really liked it.

Perhaps that was when the thought first came to me: “The world is so big. I want to see more of it.” I started seriously considering postgraduate study abroad. After doing some homework, I learnt about coursework and research master’s degrees. I had a long conversation with C, who strongly encouraged me to apply to UNSW. He even emailed his own PhD supervisor to ask whether there might be an MPhil opportunity for me. Unfortunately, his supervisor had no places left, so the idea had to wait.

The following year, my second journal paper was published. Seeing that new result rekindled my wish to apply for an MPhil. C encouraged me again: “You have research results now. Be bold and start contacting supervisors!” And that was how my search began in earnest.

Over time, I sent nearly ten emails to potential supervisors. One academic, A, replied, but she had already returned to China to teach and could not supervise me :(. Still, she kindly recommended B. I contacted B next, but was politely turned down because of my undergraduate background. By coincidence, B is now the PhD supervisor of a student from the year above me at my undergraduate university. Under C’s influence, that student also went to UNSW for a master’s degree and is now doing a fully funded PhD there. That’s something I hope to work towards too.

For a while, a research degree started to feel out of reach. My coursework offer had arrived by then. I joined a few new-student group chats through Xiaohongshu and spent time chatting there. That was how I met Z, who also wanted to apply for an MPhil. We became comrades in the application process, searching the UNSW website together and encouraging each other to keep looking for supervisors. In April last year, Z found a supervisor. Meanwhile, C’s PhD supervisor recommended R to me. Unfortunately, our research interests were not a close fit. After about a month of conversations, the contact gradually faded.

The real turning point came during a casual chat. Half joking, I said to Z, “What if I emailed your supervisor?” Z immediately asked for my CV and sent it to the supervisor privately. To my surprise, the reply was: “Hi Z, I’ve looked at his CV and he seems like a student with a lot of potential. Two students working on NLP have also just joined my group. If he’s interested, I could meet with him this Friday.”

My first thought was, “Holy shit, there’s hope!” The meeting went really well. The supervisor was willing to take me on and gave me a verbal offer. There was just one thing: my English was still pretty bad, so I needed to take an English language course first, haha.

I then came to Sydney and met A in person. A suggested a completely new research direction, very different from anything I had done before. It required a lot of mathematics, and I could not get my head around it quickly enough. That period was stressful. I was attending English classes while trying to catch up on maths at the same time. It was exhausting.

With my weak maths foundation, several of my in-person meetings in Sydney did not go as well as I had hoped. Together with the workload from the English course, this put my MPhil application on hold. By the time I finally passed the language exam, A’s student quota for the year was already full. The next opportunity would not be until the second half of 2026.

Then, through another chance encounter, I met F, a senior research student who introduced me to D. After two meetings, I finally received a verbal offer from D. With everything in place, I wrote my research proposal at lightning speed and submitted my application. After enjoying a proper two-month break, I started working with the group ahead of enrolment in early March this year. My current research is interdisciplinary and isn’t quite the NLP I did as an undergraduate, but there is a lot of common ground underneath, so I picked it up fairly quickly. I hope my first paper in this direction turns out the way I’m hoping. I’ll keep that a little secret for now.

Looking back, it has been a long road. The way ahead may not be smooth, but I believe there is plenty to look forward to!

On 24 June this year, I received my offer. In August, I also had a conference paper accepted, and I’ll soon be heading to Wuhan for the conference.

Finally, my heartfelt thanks once again to C, D, A, Z, and F, who helped me along the way!
